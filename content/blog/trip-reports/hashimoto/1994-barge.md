Most of the BARGE chips went out last week.  I had to ask a few of you
for addresses; those will probably go out on Monday.  And now the trip
report:

I flew from San Jose to Las Vegas after work on Friday with Kevan Garrett.
We got held up briefly by airport security &mdash; apparently 500 BARGE chips
looks a little suspicious when viewed through the luggage x-ray machine.
I had sent another 400 chips out with Lee Jones a day earlier, and he
told me they demanded to search his bag as well.  Dave, did they catch
you?

Our flight was marred only by the pilot locking up the brakes after
nearly overshooting the runway.  We met up with Jeff Sue and his friend
Gary who were on the same flight and I shared a taxi with them to the
Luxor.

At the Luxor, I cruised past the old poker room (still empty) over to
the much smaller, much louder poker area.  Mike Zimmers was there and
told me the Calcutta earlier that evening was a raging success.  Mike
deserves a lot of credit for putting this thing together.  I was flattered
to hear that I was the most expensive player...but more than a little
surprised to hear there was that much action.  In my opinion, that's
*really* gambling.

I made an early night of it since the poker tournament was practically
at the crack of dawn.  Lee and I met up with the Boston Poker Society
for breakfast - familiar faces Frank Irwin, Steve Blackstock, and Bill
Turner from last year, plus Andy Bloch and John Murphy.  This gang
filled me in on the rec.gambling success at the Desert Inn no-limit
tournament on Friday.  

The pre-tournament scene was a wash of new faces and names.  I saw
a lot of familiar people from last year, but the increased turnout
this year was overwhelming.  I drew my seat (#10, definitely not
one of my favorites), and tried to get rid of BARGE chips.  At least
three different people asked me if I was Yui-Bin!

The tournament gets underway and I swear I've never seen so many
rocks in one place.  After all the action I'd seen on IRC, I figured
the BARGE tournament would be bloodbath.  There were some significant
hands, but I don't think anyone was eliminated from my table before
the break.

Connie is sitting behind me in the #1 seat, and she's giving me fits.
She takes the play away from me a couple of times to win good sized
pots.  I'm holding my own in the other battles, though, and have the
best hand when I really need it.  I survive the cut to five tables,
then four, then three.  At two, we get broken up and I get sent to
sit behind the inaugural BARGE tournament champion Steve Jacobs.

I'm not in terribly comfortable chip position at this point, but
I'm managing to keep a playable stack until I pick up pocket sevens
in the small blind.  Dave Ellingson makes a small raise in late
position and I move all-in on him.  His hesitation tells me I have
the better hand, but I don't really want a call.  He eventually
calls and I toss out my sevens and say, "I have a pair," just as
the board comes down 732.  Dave shrugs and turns over AK.  The turn
is a 5, which gives him an out.  The river is a 4.

That turns out to be the last hand before the final table merge.  I
unfortunately draw the #1 seat and the small blind.  I have less than
the small blind left, so I am immediately all-in.  Dave's big blind
is enough to cover Kris Munn's and my all-ins, and he takes us out
when his 97 flops two pair.

Just like that I was suddenly just another railbird, one of the
also-rans.  There were so many people packed around the final table
I couldn't get close enough to see a thing, so I wandered around the
room meeting people, trying to gauge the progress of the tournament
by crowd reaction.  Eventually I get the word that it's heads-up between
Dave Hughes and Cliff Matthews.  Cliff dodges a few bullets but can't
overcome Dave's huge chip lead, and Dave is the new BARGE champion.

Dave was at my initial table, looking very sinister in his wrap-arounds.
From what I could tell, rec.gambling is very ably represented by its
new top dog.  Congratulations to Dave and to the other money winners
and finalists.  Thanks to Frank Irwin and Lee Jones for organizing
great tournament, I think the highlight of BARGE.

Afterwards, Spiney Norman was kind enough to chauffeur a bunch of
us over to the Desert Inn for the afternoon no-limit tournament.
We had just enough time not to be able to eat lunch, so after registering
I sat and played pai gow poker for fresh squeezed orange juice.

Thirty-three entrants start the tournament, including lots of rec.gamblers.
I start sitting across from Spiney, who fascinates me with his change
of eyeware.  First it's contacts, then dark glasses, then regular
glasses.  I'm prepared for him to pull out a monocle when I ask him
if this is some sort of strategy.  I'm somewhat disappointed to hear
it's just the dry air giving him trouble.

Connie almost proves to be my nemesis again when she survives all-in
with QJ against my AQ in the scramble for the final table, but I get
a little rush of straights to stay in contention, including a river
gut shot when all-in myself.  John Murphy appears to be on a mission
to win this thing, giving himself a constant pep talk.  Spiney looks
certain to be eliminated, then quadruples or quintuples through.  Jeff
Sue and Connie seem to have a private battle going, which Jeff wins.

At the final table Jeff and Spiney go out at #7 and #8.  I'm the
next short stack, but I triple through before the blinds get me and
we decide to whack it up six ways.  Lee and John share in the win,
but none of us wins the high card for the trophy.

At this point we run over to catch the blackjack tournament in progress.
Martin Veneroso graciously comes down to escort us up to the suite
where we find loud but organized chaos.  I can't say I fully understood
what was going on, but it looked like fun.  Thanks to Michael for
organizing it all and his staff for making it happen.

After a quick dinner, Lee and I went back to the Luxor poker room
where I killed some time dabbling in stud.  Since the pot limit game
was just under critical mass, I sat in to help them get the game
started, but I ducked out once it was going.  I'm much more comfortable
with tournament chips.  Looked like an exciting game, though, with
a *lot* of action later in the evening.

I sat in a \$1-4-8-8 hold'em game for the rest of the night, with
Mike Zimmers, Mike Maurer, Jeff Sue, and Spiney Norman at various
times.  This was a pretty tough game for low limit at the Luxor, not
helped any by the high density of rec.gamblers.  Oh, and for all
those people who claim I'm a rock, Mike Zimmers and I had a contest
to see who would see the fewest flops in half an hour.  I lost.

Sundays I always play the no-limit tournament at the Gold Coast,
and there was an invasion of rec.gamblers there as well.  I lost
my first buy on a good slow play by Lee, compounded by two mistakes
on my part.  I was eliminated when pocket nines outran my aces, a
bad beat but wouldn't have been fatal if I hadn't misplayed the
earlier hand.  So I didn't even make it to the break.

I walked around and saw Frank and Bozo with huge stacks, almost
definitely the tournament leaders at that point.  Bill Turner and
I point out to Bozo that Bill Smith, former World Series champion,
is sitting right across from him.  He seems unimpressed from behind
his mountain of chips.  But he bought the add-on anyway.

I slink away to try to kill some time and pass by Jonathan Rosenberg
at the craps table.  I'm afraid JR is going to make me put money on
the hard five, so I keep going to the pai gow poker table where he
joins me later.  We run into some dealers on a hot streak, so the
highlight of the session was seeing one player dump a full glass of
cranberry juice on another player.

Back to the tournament, and Lee, Bozo, Chuck Weinstock, and JP Massar
are still in the thick of things with two tables left.  Bozo hangs
on just long enough to make the money, and JP gets to the heads-up
and eventually finishes second.  We all retire to the Stardust for
dinner and dessert.

Chuck kindly gives us a ride to the airport afterwards and BARGE is
over for me.  I had a great time.  There is no way I'll be able to
put all the names with the new faces next year, but I'm looking
forward to seeing you there.
